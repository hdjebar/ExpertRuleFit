# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This technical report presents **ExpertRuleFit**, an open-source, scikit-learn-compatible rule-ensemble binary classifier designed for regulated decision-making environments such as credit scoring and tax-audit prioritization. The estimator combines fixed-seed gradient-boosting rule extraction (`imodels`), differential elastic-net penalty weighting via column rescaling to distinguish between mandatory "confirmatory" expert rules and "optional" expert rules, a bootstrap selection-frequency filter across $B=10$ resamples, and an unpenalised fallback refit to enforce the non-zero inclusion of confirmatory rules. In addition, the paper defines a containerized bitwise-deterministic execution envelope, implements post-fit diagnostics for sign contradictions and collinear signal absorption, evaluates seed sensitivity and penalty weights across three public credit datasets, and sketches an application design for tax-audit prioritization addressing selection bias and GDPR Article 22.

## Strengths
* The report addresses a genuine operational and governance blocker in regulated machine learning: arbitrary rule switching across random seeds and the silent elimination of legally or institutionally mandated criteria by sparse regularizers.
* The inclusion of post-fit sign-contradiction and collinear signal-absorption diagnostics (tracking variance inflation factors, correlated inactive rules, and counterfactual coefficient shifts), paired with an open-source, hash-locked containerized implementation, provides practical transparency for model validation teams.
* The presentation is commendably candid about what the empirical results do and do not show, explicitly distinguishing fixed-seed execution determinism from statistical stability under data perturbation and clearly documenting the sign-inversion artefact observed on the German Credit dataset.

## Weaknesses
* Please consider citing and differentiating against prior work that introduced the exact same name (*Expert RuleFit*) and the core mechanism of augmenting RuleFit with "confirmatory" and "optional" expert rules via differential elastic-net penalty weights, as well as established credit-scoring frameworks that enforce mandatory inclusion and sign constraints directly in the optimization objective (Reference Paper: Expert RuleFit: Complementing Rule Ensembles with Expert Knowledge, Luisa Ebner, Malte Nalenz, Annette ten Teije, Frank van Harmelen, and Thomas Augustin, 2021; Reference Paper: Learning Optimized Risk Scores, Berk Ustun and Cynthia Rudin, 2019; Reference Paper: FasterRisk: Fast and Accurate Interpretable Risk Scores, Jiachang Liu, Chudi Zhong, Boxuan Li, Margo Seltzer, and Cynthia Rudin, 2022).
* The mathematical and implementational specification of Algorithm 1 requires clarification regarding how Step 10 ("Refit with $C$ unpenalised") is executed in `scikit-learn`'s `LogisticRegressionCV(solver='saga')` without either dropping regularization globally on all kept rules or reintroducing the severe $1/\sqrt{w_c}$ ill-conditioning and non-convergence artefacts documented for small penalty weights.
* Several details in the training and diagnostic pipeline warrant closer examination, including whether `imodels.RuleFit`'s internal single-run Lasso prunes the tree rules upstream in Step 1 before the bootstrap filter runs, across-fold duplicate-row leakage when running 3-fold CV inside $n$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap draws with replacement (rather than $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ complementary subsamples without replacement), and the switch from elastic-net to ridge in the counterfactual absorption refits (Reference Paper: Stability selection, Nicolai Meinshausen and Peter Bühlmann, 2010; Reference Paper: Variable selection with error control: another look at stability selection, Rajen D. Shah and Richard J. Samworth, 2013).
* While hardcoding `seed = 42` and ignoring `random_state` deters casual seed sweeping ("X-hacking"), a regulatory auditor would note that it does not eliminate procedural arbitrariness across the Rashomon set or prevent specification shopping via input row-order permutations unless paired with canonical row/column sorting, base-seed sensitivity quantification, and data-perturbation stability benchmarks (Reference Paper: Model Multiplicity: Opportunities, Concerns, and Solutions, Emily Black, Manish Raghavan, and Solon Barocas, 2022; Reference Paper: Predictive Multiplicity in Classification, Charles Marx, Flavio du Pin Calmon, and Berk Ustun, 2020; Reference Paper: X-Hacking: The Threat of Misguided AutoML, Rahul Sharma, Sumantrak Mukherjee, Andrea Šipka, Eyke Hüllermeier, Sebastian Josef Vollmer, Sergey Redyuk, and David Antony Selby, 2025).
* To ensure the planned perturbation protocol and sign-constrained confirmatory block are credible at minimum computational cost, the authors might consider pre-filtering candidate tree rules that are heavily collinear with confirmatory rules (so box constraints do not simply project confirmatory rules to the $\beta_c = 0$ boundary), using stratified 90% subsamples or repeated stratified k-fold CV rather than 100 outer bootstraps, and reporting chance-corrected and semantic rule-coverage stability metrics alongside SIRUS (Reference Paper: SIRUS: Stable and interpretable RUle set for classification, Clément Bénard, Gérard Biau, Sébastien da Veiga, and Erwan Scornet, 2021; Reference Paper: On the Stability of Feature Selection Algorithms, Sarah Nogueira, Konstantinos Sechidis, and Gavin Brown, 2018; Reference Paper: On the Stability of Feature Selection in the Presence of Feature Correlations, Konstantinos Sechidis, Konstantinos Papangelou, Sarah Nogueira, James Weatherall, and Gavin Brown, 2019).
* In Section 7 (tax-audit prioritization), the brief treatments of selection bias and GDPR Article 22 would be much stronger if they addressed how Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) sample weights propagate through all stages of Algorithm 1 without variance explosion or MNAR bias, and updated the GDPR Article 22 discussion to reflect the binding CJEU *SCHUFA* ruling (Case C-634/21) and Member State statutory authorization under Article 22(2)(b) GDPR (Reference Paper: Reject Inference in Credit Scoring, Jonathan Crook and John Banasik, 2004; Reference Paper: Integrating Reward Maximization and Population Estimation: Sequential Decision-Making for Internal Revenue Service Audit Selection, Peter Henderson, Ben Chugg, Brandon Anderson, Kristen Altenburger, Alex Turk, John Guyton, Jacob Goldin, and Daniel E. Ho, 2023; Reference Paper: Judgment of the Court in Case C-634/21: OQ v Land Hessen (SCHUFA Holding AG), Court of Justice of the European Union, 2023).

### [Method, Standardisation & Penalty-Weighting Derivation, Algorithm 1 & Diagnostics] (Pages: 2-4)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

* **Verification Summary of Standardisation and Penalty Weighting (Section 3, Page 3):** The algebraic derivation of differential elastic-net regularisation via column rescaling is mathematically sound. Let $z_j^{\text{std}} = (z_j - m_j)/s_j$ denote the unit-scale column (where $m_j = \bar{z}_j$ for linear terms and $m_j = 0$ for binary rule columns) and $\tilde{z}_j = z_j^{\text{std}} / \sqrt{w_j}$ denote the rescaled column passed to the solver. Invariance of the linear predictor ($\sum_j \tilde{\beta}_j \tilde{z}_j = \sum_j (\tilde{\beta}_j / \sqrt{w_j}) z_j^{\text{std}} = \sum_j \beta_j^{\text{std}} z_j^{\text{std}}$) implies that the solver's fitted coefficient satisfies $\tilde{\beta}_j = \sqrt{w_j} \beta_j^{\text{std}} = s_j \sqrt{w_j} \beta_j$. Substituting $\tilde{\beta}_j$ into `scikit-learn`'s uniform elastic-net penalty $\lambda \big[\rho \sum_j |\tilde{\beta}_j| + \frac{1-\rho}{2}\sum_j \tilde{\beta}_j^2\big]$ yields an effective $\ell_1$ penalty proportional to $\sqrt{w_j}|\beta_j^{\text{std}}|$ and an effective $\ell_2$ penalty proportional to $w_j (\beta_j^{\text{std}})^2$ across all values of `l1_ratio` ($\rho \in \{0.1, \dots, 1\}$), reducing the $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ penalties on confirmatory rules by factors of $10$ and $100$ respectively at $w_c = 10^{-2}$. Mapping back to the original feature scale via $\beta_j = \tilde{\beta}_j / (s_j \sqrt{w_j})$ and $\beta_0 = \tilde{\beta}_0 - \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j m_j$ leaves predicted probabilities exactly invariant. However, several substantive specification gaps, library-interaction edge cases, and diagnostic formulations in Section 3 (pages 2–4) warrant clarification or revision, presented below in order of priority.

1. **Priority 1 — Underspecification and Failure Modes of Step 10 ("Refit with $C$ unpenalised") in `scikit-learn` (Algorithm 1 & Definition 1, Page 3)**
   * *Potential Concern:* In Algorithm 1, Line 8 fits a final elastic-net `LogisticRegressionCV` on the kept columns of $(Z - m)/(s\sqrt{w})$. Line 9 checks `if some c \in C has \beta_c = 0 then` (corresponding to $|\beta_c| \le 10^{-10}$ under Definition 1) and Line 10 states: `Refit with C unpenalised`. However, `scikit-learn`'s `LogisticRegression` and `LogisticRegressionCV` (using the `saga` solver) do not natively expose a per-feature `penalty_factor` vector (unlike R's `glmnet`, where setting `penalty.factor = 0` leaves only subset $C$ unpenalised while maintaining the tuned elastic-net penalty on all other columns). Furthermore, under the column-rescaling parameterization $\tilde{z}_j = (z_j - m_j)/(s_j \sqrt{w_j})$, setting $w_c = 0$ is undefined (division by zero), and the text on Page 3 explicitly shows that tiny weights ($w_c = 10^{-8}$) cause severe ill-conditioning ($1/\sqrt{w_c} = 10^4$) that makes `saga` stop far from the optimum without a convergence warning. Consequently, a reader cannot determine from Section 3 or Algorithm 1 how Line 10 is mathematically or algorithmically executed:
     1. *Global vs. subset unpenalised refit:* If Line 10 disables regularisation globally (`penalty=None`) across *all* columns retained at Line 7 (or all active columns from Line 8 plus $C$), then all retained tree rules, optional rules, and linear terms lose their shrinkage penalty whenever a single confirmatory rule hits zero. Any non-confirmatory columns that passed Line 7 ($\ge \tau B$) but were zeroed out by Line 8 would re-enter the model unpenalised, and collinear tree rules risk severe coefficient inflation. Conversely, if Line 10 attempts to unpenalise *only* $C$ by shrinking $w_c \ll 10^{-2}$ (or via a two-stage offset) while keeping other kept columns penalised with an $\ell_1$ component, it either re-introduces the `saga` conditioning failure documented on Page 3 or alters the regularisation path on the remaining columns, potentially driving previously active non-confirmatory rules from Line 8 (or another collinear confirmatory rule $c' \in C$) to zero during the refit.
     2. *Collinearity detection under `saga`:* The prose under Definition 1 states that *"if a rule is still excluded (perfect collinearity or a constant column), the flag `confirmatory_all_active_` is false."* Because `saga` is a first-order stochastic average gradient solver initialized at zero, two perfectly collinear non-zero columns receive identical gradients and split the coefficient weight equally ($\beta_{c_1} = \beta_{c_2} \neq 0$) rather than setting one column to $|\beta_c| \le 10^{-10}$; thus, perfect collinearity will not generally set $\beta_c = 0$ unless collinear columns are explicitly detected via a rank or QR check.
     3. *Boundary interaction with the planned sign-constrained block:* When moving to the planned convex sign-constrained, unpenalised confirmatory block, note that under standard non-negativity/non-positivity box constraints ($s_c \beta_c \ge 0$), any confirmatory rule whose partial correlation with $y$ has the opposite sign will be projected directly onto the boundary $\beta_c = 0$, violating Definition 1 ($|\beta_c| > 10^{-10}$) unless a strict interior margin $s_c \beta_c^{\text{std}} \ge \epsilon > 0$ or collinear-rule pruning is enforced.
   * *Concrete Fix:* Explicitly state in Section 3 and Line 10 of Algorithm 1 the exact optimisation objective, active feature support (whether Step 10 runs on the columns surviving Step 7 or freezes the non-zero support of Step 8 plus $C$), and solver configuration used for the fallback refit. Also specify whether the threshold $|\beta_c| > 10^{-10}$ in Definition 1 is evaluated on the original scale $\beta_c$, the standardised scale $\beta_c^{\text{std}}$, or the solver scale $\tilde{\beta}_c$. For the planned sign-constrained confirmatory block, replace the `saga` column-rescaling workaround and fallback refit with cyclical coordinate descent supporting native per-feature penalty factors $v_j \ge 0$ ($v_c = 0$ for $c \in C$) and box constraints $l_j \le \beta_j \le u_j$—as in Friedman, Hastie, and Tibshirani (2010, *"Regularization Paths for Generalized Linear Models via Coordinate Descent"*, *Journal of Statistical Software*, 33(1), 1–22)—or bounded quasi-Newton (`scipy.optimize.minimize(method='L-BFGS-B')`).

2. **Priority 2 — Upstream Lasso Selection Inside `imodels` RuleFit in Step 1 (Algorithm 1, Line 1, Page 3)**
   * *Potential Concern:* Line 1 of Algorithm 1 states: *"Fit gradient boosting (250 trees, depth 4, seed 42) and extract at most 50 rules $R$ via `imodels` RuleFit"*. A binary gradient-boosting ensemble of 250 trees of depth 4 generates up to $250 \times (2^{4+1} - 2) = 7{,}500$ internal and terminal node conditions. In `imodels.RuleFit` (Singh et al., 2021), capping the output at `max_rules=50` does not merely extract 50 frequent rules from a 250-tree ensemble: depending on how `imodels` is called, `RuleFit.fit()` either constrains the tree generator to grow tiny trees whose total node count is at most 50, or executes its own internal $\ell_1$-penalised cross-validated GLM (`LassoCV` / `LogisticRegressionCV`) over the full dictionary of tree nodes to select at most 50 active rules before returning them.      2. Because expert rules $C$ and $O$ are appended only in Line 2, the 50 tree rules in $R$ are pre-selected in Line 1 without accounting for collinearity or signal overlap with $C$ and $O$.

3. **Priority 3 — Duplicate-Row Cross-Validation Leakage in Bootstrap Draws ($B = 10$) and Inner Solver Seeding (Algorithm 1, Lines 3–8, Page 3)**
   * *Potential Concern:* In Algorithm 1, Line 4 draws $B = 10$ bootstrap samples (`seed 42 + b`), and Line 5 fits `LogisticRegressionCV` (3-fold CV over $\ell_1$ ratios in $\{0.1, \dots, 1\}$) on each draw:
     1. *In-fold duplicate leakage under bootstrap sampling with replacement:* Standard stability selection (Meinshausen and Bühlmann, 2010, *JRSS-B*, 72(4), 417–473; Shah and Samworth, 2013, *JRSS-B*, 75(1), 55–80) uses subsampling of size $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ *without replacement* (or complementary pairs) rather than $n$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap sampling *with replacement*. Drawing $n$ rows with replacement produces roughly $1 - e^{-1} \approx 63.2\%$ unique rows and $\approx 36.8\%$ duplicate rows. When an $n$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap sample containing duplicate rows is partitioned by a standard 3-fold `StratifiedKFold` splitter in Line 5, identical training observations are placed into both the training fold and the validation fold, causing across-fold data leakage that biases `LogisticRegressionCV` toward under-regularised (denser) models during the frequency-filtering stage.
     2. *Omitted inner seeding and edge-case specifications:* Several procedural details required to reproduce Lines 2–8 from the text are not stated: whether the bootstrap draw in Line 4 is stratified by $y$; how zero-variance candidate columns ($s_j = 0$ in the training set or within a bootstrap subsample/CV fold) are handled prior to dividing by $s_j \sqrt{w_j}$; what grid of inverse regularisation parameters (`Cs`) and CV scoring rule are used by `LogisticRegressionCV`; and how random seeds are assigned to the 3-fold CV fold splitters and the stochastic `saga` solver in Lines 5, 8, and 10.
   * *Concrete Fix:* Consider replacing the $n$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap with replacement in Line 4 by stratified subsampling without replacement—ideally $B=25$ complementary pairs of size $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ (50 inner fits on half the rows) as in Shah and Samworth (2013)—which simultaneously eliminates duplicate-row CV leakage in Line 5, halves the sample size per `saga` fit, and provides theoretical error bounds. Additionally, add a brief specification below Algorithm 1 stating the `Cs` grid, the zero-variance column handling ($s_j = 0$), and the exact seed schedule passed to the 3-fold CV splitters and `saga` solver in Lines 5, 8, and 10.

4. **Priority 4 — Mathematical Specification of the Absorption Diagnostics: Penalty-Family Shift and Uncentred VIF (Section 3, Page 4)**
   * *Potential Concern:* Under *"Sign check and absorption diagnostics"* (Page 4), two mathematical details of the diagnostic definitions can distort the reported absorption metrics:
     1. *Switching from Elastic-Net to Ridge in counterfactual refits:* The text states that the estimator reports *"the change in the coefficients of retained correlated columns when the confirmatory rule is removed (ridge refits with the final model's $C$ and the same weights)."* First, using the symbol $C$ here to denote `scikit-learn`'s inverse regularisation hyperparameter ($C = 1/\lambda$) collides notationally with $C$ denoting the set of confirmatory rules in Algorithm 1. More importantly, the deployed model in Line 8 is fitted with an **elastic-net** penalty (`l1_ratio` $\rho^* \in \{0.1, \dots, 1\}$), whereas the diagnostic refit switches to **ridge** ($\rho = 0$). Comparing the deployed elastic-net coefficients against a ridge refit without the confirmatory rule—or even comparing two matched ridge refits (with and without $c \in C$) at the same scalar inverse penalty—conflates the removal of rule $c$ with a change in penalty geometry from $\rho^*$ to $\rho = 0$. In particular, under the rescaling $\tilde{z}_c = z_c / (s_c \sqrt{w_c})$ with $w_c = 10^{-2}$, an $\ell_1$ penalty weights the confirmatory rule by $\sqrt{w_c} = 0.1$ whereas a pure $\ell_2$ penalty weights it by $w_c = 0.01$, redistributing weight across collinear rules purely because of the shift from elastic-net to ridge.
     2. *VIF computation on uncentred binary rule columns:* The estimator reports each confirmatory rule's *"variance inflation factor among retained columns."* Because binary rule columns are not centred ($m_j = 0$, Page 3), computing VIF via uncentred inner products $(Z_{\text{kept}}^\top Z_{\text{kept}})^{-1}$ (as happens by default in standard routines such as `statsmodels.stats.outliers_influence.variance_inflation_factor` unless an explicit constant column is prepended) computes the uncentred $R_c^2$, which conflates baseline rule activation frequency (mean $> 0$) with collinearity and severely inflates VIF values.
   * *Concrete Fix:* (a) Rename the inverse regularisation parameter (e.g., $C_{\text{reg}}$ or $\lambda^{-1}$) to avoid colliding with the confirmatory rule set $C$; (b) clarify whether the absorption counterfactual compares two matched refits (with and without $c$) and consider performing those refits at the final model's exact selected `(C_reg, l1_ratio)` pair on the fixed retained support so that penalty-family shifts do not confound signal absorption; and (c) specify explicitly that VIF among retained columns is computed from the centred correlation matrix (i.e., OLS regression of column $c$ on the other retained columns *including an intercept*).

#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Failure Mode of the Planned Sign-Constrained Confirmatory Block Under Multicollinearity (Section 5, p. 5, Section 6, p. 6 & Section 8, p. 7)**
   * **Potential concern:** In Section 5 (*Penalty-weight sensitivity (German Credit)*, p. 5), the report documents that the confirmatory rule `"checking account below 0 DM"` received a negative coefficient in every converged fit ($w_c \in \{10^{-4}, 10^{-2}, 1\}$), contradicting the expected positive default-risk direction because *"the model also retains the ordinal checking-status variable and tree rules containing the same condition, which carry the effect."* In Section 6 (*No sign constraint*, p. 6) and Section 8 (p. 7), the planned fix is described as *"a sign-constrained, unpenalised confirmatory block"* solved via bounded optimization. A potential concern is that enforcing a convex sign/box constraint ($s_c \beta_c \ge 0$ or $s_c \beta_c \ge \epsilon > 0$, where $s_c \in \{+1, -1\}$ is the expected sign) without resolving the underlying structural collinearity in the augmented matrix $Z$ will introduce two new optimization and auditability failure modes:
     1. *Boundary Pinning vs. Definition 1 (Inclusion):* If the unconstrained optimum of the strictly convex penalized negative log-likelihood places $s_c \hat{\beta}_c < 0$ because the retained ordinal feature and overlapping tree rules already over-explain the target log-odds on the subgroup where $c(x)=1$, KKT complementary slackness under the half-space projection $s_c \beta_c \ge 0$ will drive the confirmatory coefficient directly to the boundary $\hat{\beta}_c = 0$. This directly violates **Definition 1 (Inclusion: $|\beta_c| > 10^{-10}$)** and sets `confirmatory_all_active_ = False`.
     2. *Cosmetic Inclusion or Compensatory Cancellation:* If a strict positive lower bound $s_c \beta_c \ge \epsilon > 0$ (e.g., $\epsilon = 10^{-9}$) is enforced to satisfy Definition 1, the solver will pin $\hat{\beta}_c = s_c \epsilon$—passing both Definition 1 and `confirmatory_sign_ok_` while contributing virtually zero log-odds to predictions (a purely cosmetic inclusion that a model auditor inspecting coefficient magnitudes will reject). Conversely, if a larger minimum floor $\epsilon_{\text{min}}$ is enforced (or if the unpenalized confirmatory block is fitted jointly) while overlapping tree rules (e.g., `checking < 0 DM AND age > 25`) and the parent linear term remain unconstrained, the solver will shift those collinear terms in the opposite direction to compensate, splitting the net marginal effect of the domain condition across opposing terms.
   * **Concrete Fixes:** Pair the planned sign-constrained solver with a **deterministic structural deduplication and collinearity-pruning step** prior to the bootstrap loop (between Steps 2 and 3 of Algorithm 1):
     1. *Subsumption and Correlation Filtering Against $C$:* Before running the bootstrap selection fits, automatically prune from the candidate tree rules $R$ any single-condition rule that splits on the same feature and threshold direction as a confirmatory rule $c \in C$, as well as any candidate rule whose training-set binary activation vector exceeds a strict correlation or overlap threshold with $c$ (e.g., $|r(r_j, c)| \ge 0.70\text{--}0.90$ or Jaccard overlap $\ge 0.85$).
     2. *Handling Redundant Raw Encodings / Two-Stage Offset Fitting:* When a confirmatory rule thresholds a raw feature $x_j$ (especially an ordinally encoded categorical feature such as `checking_status`), provide an option either to exclude/residualize the competing raw column $x_j$ from $Z$, or to perform a **two-stage offset fit** where the unpenalized, sign-constrained confirmatory block $C$ is fitted first via bounded logistic regression and its linear predictor $\eta_C = Z_C \hat{\beta}_C$ is passed as a fixed offset into the penalized selection of $R \cup O$. For established domain- and sign-constrained credit risk-score formulations, see **Ustun and Rudin (2019)** (*"Learning Optimized Risk Scores"*, *Journal of Machine Learning Research*, 20(150), 1–75), **Liu et al. (2022)** (*"FasterRisk: Fast and Accurate Interpretable Risk Scores"*, *NeurIPS*, Vol. 35, 17903–17916), and **Chen et al. (2022)** (*"A Holistic Approach to Interpretability in Financial Lending: Models, Visualizations, and Summary-Explanations"*, *Decision Support Systems*, 152, 113647, `arXiv:2106.02390`).

2. **Experimental Design for the Proposed Perturbation, Ablation, and Calibration Protocol (Section 5, pp. 5–6, Section 6, p. 6 & Section 8, p. 7)**
   * **Potential concern:** The *Proposed perturbation protocol* at the bottom of page 5 (and summarized in Section 8) outlines drawing 100 bootstrap or 90% subsamples of the training set, fitting fixed-seed `RuleFit` and `ExpertRuleFit`, reporting mean pairwise Jaccard similarity and per-rule selection frequencies as in Bénard et al. (2021), reporting AUC with confidence intervals over repeated splits, and including `SIRUS` and $\ell_2$-penalized logistic regression as baselines. As currently formulated, four methodological blind spots could undermine the credibility of the stability claim or create unnecessary computational cost:
     1. *Threshold-Jitter Blind Spot of Exact-String Jaccard on Continuous Splits:* In `SIRUS` (**Bénard et al., 2021**, *Electronic Journal of Statistics*, 15(1), 427–505), continuous features are pre-discretized onto fixed empirical deciles ($q=10$), so extracted rules share identical string representations across subsamples. In `RuleFit` and `ExpertRuleFit`, however, gradient boosting splits on continuous standardized values. Across two 90% subsamples, a split threshold will shift slightly (e.g., `duration > 24.0` in subsample 1 vs. `duration > 24.5` in subsample 2). Exact-string Jaccard similarity treats these two rules as completely disjoint (0 similarity), artificially collapsing the measured stability of both `RuleFit` and `ExpertRuleFit` even when the two rules fire on 99% of the exact same test instances.
     2. *Cardinality Bias of Raw Jaccard Similarity:* Unadjusted pairwise Jaccard similarity scales upward with the proportion of candidate columns selected: a method retaining 42–48 active terms out of $\approx 62\text{--}73$ candidates will achieve a much higher Jaccard score by chance alone than a sparser model like `SIRUS` (typically 10–15 rules).
     3. *Outer Bootstrap Duplication and Preprocessing Leakage:* If the outer perturbation loop uses $n$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap resampling on top of `ExpertRuleFit`'s inner bootstrap (Step 4 of Algorithm 1), unique sample coverage in the inner fits drops to $\approx 40\%\text{--}46\%$, inducing duplicate-row ties and degrading tree quality on smaller datasets like German Credit ($n_{\text{train}} = 800$). Moreover, the protocol does not explicitly state that imputation medians/modes, ordinal encodings, and standardization scales ($m_j, s_j$) must be refitted *inside* each perturbed subsample rather than inherited from the parent 80% split.
     4. *Post-Hoc Calibration vs. Log-Odds Additivity:* Section 6 (*Calibration not evaluated*, p. 6) notes that optional 5-fold Platt scaling or isotonic regression exists and that reliability curves and Brier scores will be reported. Applying non-parametric *isotonic regression* post-hoc introduces a piecewise-constant step transformation that breaks the direct additive log-odds decomposition $\operatorname{logit}(P(y=1|x)) = \beta_0 + \sum_j \beta_j z_j(x)$, meaning individual coefficients $\beta_j$ can no longer be audited as constant log-odds contributions.
   * **Concrete Fixes (Minimum-Cost Credible Protocol):**
     1. *Resampling Scheme:* Use **$M = 25\text{--}50$ stratified 90% subsamples without replacement** of the training set (or **$10 \times 5$-fold / $5 \times 10$-fold repeated stratified cross-validation**, matching **Bénard et al., 2021**), optionally paired with a **$1\%$-row deletion sensitivity test** (**Black, Yeom, and Fredrikson, 2021**, *"Leave-one-out Unfairness"*, *ACM FAccT*), strictly refitting all preprocessing (imputation, ordinal encoding, standardization) and tree extraction inside each outer training split while evaluating predictions and rule activations on a common held-out test set. ($M = 25\text{--}50$ cuts compute cost by $2\times\text{--}4\times$ vs. $M = 100$ while keeping standard errors on stability indices below $0.02$).
     2. *Three-Tier Stability Metrics (Syntactic, Semantic Coverage, and Predictive):* Alongside exact-string Jaccard and the **Sørensen–Dice** similarity index $2|A \cap B|/(|A|+|B|)$ (the exact metric reported in **Bénard et al., 2021**) after canonicalizing clause order, report:
        * **Chance-Corrected Feature/Antecedent Stability ($\hat{\Phi}$):** Apply the chance-corrected selection stability estimator with closed-form confidence intervals of **Nogueira, Sechidis, and Brown (2018)** (*"On the Stability of Feature Selection Algorithms"*, *Journal of Machine Learning Research*, 18(174), 1–54) and its correlation-aware extension (**Sechidis et al., 2019**, *"On the Stability of Feature Selection in the Presence of Feature Correlations"*, *ECML PKDD*, pp. 327–342), evaluated over both active rule strings and active feature-antecedent sets (ignoring minor continuous threshold shifts).
        * **Semantic / Rule-Coverage Stability on $X_{\text{test}}$:** Evaluate each selected rule on the held-out test set $X_{\text{test}}$ to obtain its binary activation vector $r(X_{\text{test}}) \in \{0,1\}^{n_{\text{test}}}$, and compute the mean optimal bipartite matching (via the Hungarian algorithm) of pairwise Jaccard similarities between the active rules' sample-coverage vectors. This separates benign continuous threshold jitter from true structural instability.
        * **Predictive Multiplicity / Score Stability:** Report pairwise Spearman rank correlation, mean absolute prediction difference across the $M$ perturbed fits on $X_{\text{test}}$, and prediction ambiguity/discrepancy rates (**Marx, Calmon, and Ustun, 2020**, *"Predictive Multiplicity in Classification"*, *ICML*, PMLR 119, 6765–6774).
     3. *Factorial Ablation and Baseline Matrix:* Evaluate a clean progression on the same resamples to isolate which mechanism drives stability and discrimination:
        * **(A0) Fixed-Seed `RuleFit` (`random_state=42`):** Standard single Lasso fit without bootstrap filtering or expert rules.
        * **(A1) Fixed-Seed `RuleFit` + Penalty-Weighted Expert Rules ($B=0/1$ or $\tau=0$, no bootstrap filter):** Isolates the adaptive penalty ($w_c, w_o$) and mandatory refit alone—and allows direct comparison against **Ebner et al. (2021)** (*"Expert RuleFit: Complementing Rule Ensembles with Expert Knowledge"*, *AIME Workshop KR4HC 2021*).
        * **(A2) `ExpertRuleFit` without Expert Rules ($B=10, \tau=0.8, C=\emptyset, O=\emptyset$):** Isolates the standalone stabilization effect of the 10-draw frequency filter (**Meinshausen and Bühlmann, 2010**, *JRSS-B*, 72(4), 417–473; **Shah and Samworth, 2013**, *JRSS-B*, 75(1), 55–80).
        * **(A3 & A4) Full `ExpertRuleFit` (Unconstrained vs. Sign-Constrained + Deduplicated Confirmatory Block):** Verifies that sign contradictions are eliminated without hurting test AUC.
        * **(External Baselines):** Alongside **SIRUS** (**Bénard et al., 2021**), $\ell_1$- and $\ell_2$-penalized logistic regression on raw features + expert rules (testing whether tree rules add value over linear terms alone), consider adding a compressed or domain-constrained scorecard baseline such as **CRE** (**Nalenz and Augustin, 2022**, *AISTATS*, PMLR 151), **OptBinning** (**Navas-Palencia, 2020**, *"Optimal Binning: Mathematical Programming Formulation"*, `arXiv:2001.08025`), **RiskSLIM** (**Ustun and Rudin, 2019**, *JMLR*), or **FasterRisk** (**Liu et al., 2022**, *NeurIPS*), and evaluating confirmatory sign constraints on the public **FICO HELOC** dataset (**Chen et al., 2022**, *Decision Support Systems*), which provides established credit-bureau monotonicity directions for all 23 features.
     4. *Calibration and Local Explanation Complexity:* Report test-set **Brier score**, **log-loss**, and **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)** (10 quantile bins) for the uncalibrated model, an unpenalized/relaxed refit on the retained rule support (cf. **Fokkema, 2020**, *Journal of Statistical Software*, 92(12)), and 5-fold **Platt scaling** (noting that linear Platt scaling $\sigma(a \cdot \eta + b)$ with $a > 0$ rescales all coefficients proportionally without breaking relative log-odds additivity, whereas isotonic regression breaks constant per-rule log-odds attributions), alongside the mean number of active rules firing per test case (**Kanamori, 2023**, *ECML PKDD*).

3. **Diagnosing the ~2,100-Fit `saga` Bottleneck and Determinism-Preserving Speedups (Section 5, p. 5, Section 6, p. 6 & Section 8, p. 7)**
   * **Potential concern:** Section 5 (p. 5) and Section 6 (*Runtime*, p. 6) report that a single fit executes roughly 2,100 `saga` fits single-threaded ($(B = 10 \text{ bootstraps} + 1 \text{ final fit}) \times 3 \text{ CV folds} \times 7\text{--}10 \text{ } \ell_1\text{-ratios} \times 10 \text{ } C \text{ path values} = 2,310\text{--}3,300$ path evaluations), taking 10 minutes on German Credit ($800$ training rows) and 3.5 hours on HMEQ ($4,768$ training rows and $\le 70$ columns). At 3.5 hours per fit, running even 50 perturbation draws on HMEQ would require 175 CPU-hours. For a design matrix of dimension $4,768 \times 70$, 2,100 elastic-net path steps take hours only because of specific numerical and grid bottlenecks in how `saga` is invoked:
     1. *Un-centered Sparse Rule Columns & Row-Norm Ill-Conditioning for `saga`:* `scikit-learn`'s `saga` is an incremental stochastic gradient solver whose step size and convergence rate depend directly on the maximum squared row norm $\max_i \|\tilde{z}_i\|_2^2$. Leaving binary rule columns un-centered (keeping $<50$ rule columns sparse saves $<3\text{ MB}$ of RAM on 4,768 rows while adding `scipy.sparse` indexing overhead and collinearity with the intercept) and scaling columns by $1/(s_j \sqrt{w_j})$ with $1/\sqrt{w_c} = 10$ severely degrades `saga` convergence on correlated rules.
     2. *Tight Benchmark Tolerance ($10^{-6}$) and Redundant Inner-Bootstrap Grid Search:* Running a full 2D CV grid across all $\ell_1$-ratios at `tol = 1e-6` inside each of the $B=10$ bootstrap screening draws—where Steps 3–6 only record binary support indicator vectors ($\hat{\beta}_j \neq 0$)—wastes the vast majority of gradient epochs at the weak-regularization end of the $C$ path.
   * **Concrete Fixes (100% Bitwise-Deterministic Within the Container Envelope):**
     1. *Coarse-to-Fine Grid and Tolerance in Inner Bootstraps:* In Step 5 of Algorithm 1 (the $B=10$ bootstrap frequency filter), use the library default `tol = 1e-4` and restrict the inner $\ell_1$-ratio grid to 2 or 3 representative values (e.g., $\{0.5, 0.8, 1.0\}$), reserving the full $\ell_1$-ratio grid for the single final fit in Step 8. This cuts total solver iterations by ~70%–85% while remaining 100% bitwise deterministic.
     2. *Pre-Bootstrap Duplicate Rule Pruning & Dense C-Contiguous Centering:* A depth-4 tree ensemble capped at 50 rules frequently extracts syntactically distinct rules that produce identical binary indicator columns on the training data. Deduplicating identical binary columns in $Z$ (breaking ties deterministically by lexicographical rule string or column index) prior to Step 3 and passing a dense, centered `float64` matrix to the solver (mapping the intercept back via $\beta_0 \leftarrow \beta_0 - \sum_j \beta_j m_j$, as $(Z-m)/(s\sqrt{w})$ in Algorithm 1 already implies) eliminates exact collinearity and accelerates per-epoch throughput.
     3. *Deterministic Process-Level Parallelism Across the $B=10$ Bootstraps:* Because each bootstrap iteration $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$ uses an independent fixed seed ($42 + b$) and runs with single-threaded BLAS (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`), dispatching the $B=10$ draws across parallel worker processes (`joblib.Parallel(n_jobs=K, backend="loky")`) and aggregating selection counts in fixed index order $b = 1, \dots, B$ produces results **bitwise identical** to sequential execution in $1/\min(B, K)$ wall-clock time.
     4. *Pathwise Coordinate Descent Alternative:* Replacing `saga` with deterministic cyclical coordinate descent with active-set screening and warm starts (**Friedman, Hastie, and Tibshirani, 2010**, *"Regularization Paths for Generalized Linear Models via Coordinate Descent"*, *Journal of Statistical Software*, 33(1), 1–22; or Dual Extrapolation / `celer` by **Massias, Gramfort, and Salmon, 2018**, *"Celer: a Fast Solver for the Lasso with Dual Extrapolation"*, *ICML*, PMLR 80, 3315–3324) solves a $4,768 \times 70$ regularization path in milliseconds and natively supports per-feature penalty factors ($v_c = 0$ for unpenalized rules, avoiding $1/\sqrt{w_c}$ ill-conditioning) and coordinate-wise box/sign constraints ($\beta_c \ge 0$).

### [Tax-Audit Prioritisation Design, Selection Bias, GDPR Article 22 & Conclusions] (Pages: 6-7)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Statistical and Algorithmic Integration of Inverse-Probability Weighting (IPW) for Selection Bias (Section 7, pages 6–7; Section 8, page 7)**
   * *Potential Concern:* Section 7 states that because outcomes exist only for audited files, *"a small random audit sample and inverse-probability weighting are needed,"* and Section 8 lists *"handling of selection bias when labels come only from past audits"* as future work. While pairing a random audit sample with IPW is the appropriate conceptual starting point for the selective-labels problem (Lakkaraju et al., 2017, *KDD*; Kleinberg et al., 2018, *Quarterly Journal of Economics*), three concrete statistical and architectural considerations arise when integrating sample weights into `ExpertRuleFit` (Algorithm 1):
     1. **Positivity Violations and Variance Inflation:** IPW requires strict positivity ($P(S_i = 1 \mid X_i) > 0$ for all taxpayer strata $X_i$, where $S_i \in \{0,1\}$ indicates audit selection) and Missing-at-Random (MAR) selection ($Y_i \perp S_i \mid X_i$). In tax administration, historical operational audits often follow deterministic cutoff rules or unrecorded inspector heuristics, leaving large regions of the feature space with zero operational selection probability. When positivity is guaranteed solely by a *"small random audit sample"* with sampling fraction $\epsilon$ (e.g., $\epsilon = 0.01\text{--}0.02$), the inverse propensity weights $v_i = 1 / \hat{P}(S_i = 1 \mid X_i)$ in historically un-audited strata reach $50\text{--}100$. Unstabilised weights of this magnitude cause severe variance inflation in both tree splitting and $\ell_1$/elastic-net feature selection. Moreover, if inspectors historically relied on soft information not recorded in $X$ (Missing Not at Random, MNAR), standard IPW on operational audits remains biased (Crook and Banasik, 2004, *European Journal of Operational Research*; Ehrhardt et al., 2021, *European Journal of Operational Research*).
     2. **Propagation of Observation Weights Through Algorithm 1 (Risk of Double-Counting or Biased Scaling):** The blueprint does not specify how observation weights $v_i$ enter the four stages of `ExpertRuleFit`:
        * *Stage 1 (Tree Rule Extraction):* Observation weights $v_i$ must be passed as `sample_weight` to the gradient-boosting rule generator (Line 1) so that split gains reflect the target taxpayer population rather than past selection habits.
        * *Stage 2 (Standardisation and Penalty Scaling):* In Line 2, column centres $m_j$ and scales $s_j$ determine the rescaled columns $\tilde{z}_j = (z_j - m_j)/(s_j \sqrt{w_j})$ and thus directly scale the effective $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ penalties. Computing unweighted sample means and standard deviations on a heavily selected audit pool biases $s_j$ relative to the general population; $m_j$ and $s_j$ should instead be computed using IPW-weighted moments:
          $$m_j^{(v)} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n v_i z_{ij}}{\sum_{i=1}^n v_i}, \qquad s_j^{(v)} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n v_i (z_{ij} - m_j^{(v)})^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n v_i}}.$$
        * *Stage 3 (Bootstrap Frequency Filter):* In Lines 3–6, if observation weights $v_i$ are used both as sampling probabilities in the bootstrap draw (Line 4) *and* as `sample_weight` in `LogisticRegressionCV` (Line 5), the inverse propensity weights are applied twice ($v_i^2$). Conversely, if uniform unweighted bootstrap draws are used with a small random audit sample, high-weight random-audit cases are omitted from $\approx 36.8\%$ of the $B=10$ draws, which can cause genuine population rules to fail the $\tau = 0.8$ ($8/10$) selection filter.
     3. **Pilot Evaluation Metric and Cost Denominator:** Section 7 states that *"A pilot would compare the hit rate per audit hour with the current selection method on the random sample."* While the binary target $y$ in Table 3 models whether an adjustment exceeds a threshold ($P(y_i = 1 \mid x_i)$), optimizing *"hit rate per audit hour"* introduces a variable cost denominator (audit hours $h_i$). If complex returns require substantially more hours to audit, ranking purely by $\hat{P}(y_i = 1 \mid x_i)$ rather than expected yield per hour $\hat{P}(y_i = 1 \mid x_i) / \hat{\mathbb{E}}[h_i \mid x_i]$ is suboptimal. Furthermore, evaluating precision-at-$k$ retrospectively on a small random sample yields wide confidence intervals unless combined with operational audits via doubly robust policy evaluation.
   * *Concrete Fix:* In Sections 7 and 8, specify how observation weights propagate through Algorithm 1: (a) use IPW-weighted moments for $(m_j, s_j)$ in Line 2 and pass `sample_weight` to tree induction in Line 1; (b) in Line 4, draw *stratified* bootstrap resamples (stratified by audit source: random vs. operational sample) without weighting the draw probabilities, and apply `sample_weight` inside the elastic-net logistic loss in Line 5 so weights are neither double-counted nor dropped across draws; and (c) recommend **stratified random audit sampling** (as in the IRS National Research Program; Henderson et al., 2023, *AAAI* / *arXiv:2204.11910*), weight stabilisation/clipping, or **Doubly Robust / Augmented IPW (AIPW)** estimation (Robins, Rotnitzky, and Zhao, 1994, *JASA*; Dudík, Langford, and Li, 2011, *ICML*) and sample-selection corrections (Heckman, 1979, *Econometrica*).

2. **Legal and Doctrinal Precision on GDPR Article 22, Human Oversight, and Statutory Tax-Audit Exceptions (Section 7, pages 6–7)**
   * *Potential Concern:* Section 7 addresses automated decision-making by stating: *"Third, human decision: the score suggests a file and an agent decides, consistent with Article 22 GDPR on automated decisions,"* and addresses fairness by stating: *"Second, fairness and legal grounds: variables without legal basis are excluded and score distributions compared across taxpayer groups."* For a European regulatory and model-governance audience, this framing has three potential doctrinal gaps:
     1. **Impact of the CJEU *SCHUFA* Ruling (Case C-634/21) on Human-in-the-Loop Scoring:** In *OQ v Land Hessen (SCHUFA Holding AG)* (CJEU Case C-634/21, Judgment of 7 December 2023, ECLI:EU:C:2023:957), the Court of Justice of the European Union held that the automated generation of a probability score concerning an individual itself constitutes an "automated individual decision" under **Article 22(1) GDPR** whenever a downstream human or third-party decision-maker *"draws strongly"* on that score to establish, implement, or alter a legal relationship or impose a measure with similarly significant effects. Under the Article 29 Working Party / EDPB *Guidelines on Automated Individual Decision-Making and Profiling* (WP251rev.01), nominal human sign-off (*"an agent decides"*) does not place a system outside Article 22(1) unless the agent exercises *meaningful* oversight—requiring the authority, competence, time, and independent evidentiary basis to override the score without automation bias ("rubber-stamping").
     2. **Preparatory Selection vs. Article 22(2)(b) Statutory Authorization for Public Tax Authorities:** While selecting a tax return for an audit is an investigative/preparatory administrative step rather than a final automated tax assessment, an intrusive audit can still be argued to "similarly significantly affect" a taxpayer under Article 22(1). More importantly, European tax administrations typically do not rely solely on avoiding Article 22(1) via manual review; instead, automated risk profiling for tax compliance operates under the statutory exception in **Article 22(2)(b) GDPR** (alongside **Article 6(1)(e)** and **Article 23(1)(e) GDPR**), which expressly permits automated decision-making and profiling when authorized by Union or Member State law to which the controller is subject (e.g., Article 88 of the German Federal Fiscal Code / *Abgabenordnung* or equivalent national tax statutes), provided the law lays down suitable measures to safeguard the data subject's rights, freedoms, and legitimate interests. Framing `ExpertRuleFit`'s deterministic replayability and protected legal criteria (*confirmatory rules*) as technical safeguards required under **Article 22(2)(b) GDPR** makes the governance case much stronger.
     3. **Proxy Reconstruction and Error-Rate Parity vs. Marginal Score Distributions:** Excluding variables without a direct legal basis does not prevent depth-4 conjunctive tree rules or EBM pairwise interactions from reconstructing protected attributes via correlated proxies (e.g., deduction categories, occupation, or geographic features). Moreover, comparing marginal *score distributions* across taxpayer groups tests for demographic parity (equal selection rates), which can be inappropriate when true non-compliance base rates legitimately differ across economic sectors; regulators and ombudsmen instead require checking **error-rate parity / conditional calibration**—specifically comparing the **False Positive Rate** (the burden of unproductive audits imposed on compliant taxpayers) across groups on the random audit sample (Chouldechova, 2017, *Big Data*; Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan, 2017, *ITCS*).
   * *Concrete Fix:* Revise the second and third conditions in Section 7 (pages 6–7) to: (a) cite CJEU Case C-634/21 (*SCHUFA*, 2023) to clarify that human review must be substantively independent (supported by the active-rule explanations in Table 3 and contradiction diagnostics) rather than deferential to the score; (b) reference **Article 22(2)(b) and Article 6(1)(e) GDPR** regarding Member State statutory authorization and required contestability safeguards for tax administrations; and (c) specify comparing conditional false-positive audit rates (unproductive audit burden on compliant taxpayers) on the random audit sample in addition to marginal score distributions.
